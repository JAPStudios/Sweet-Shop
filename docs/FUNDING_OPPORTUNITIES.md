# Funding routes for Sweet Shop: Digital Pantry

Research date: 2026-11-26

Sweet Shop: Digital Pantry is an open online marketplace intended to let individuals and organisations sell their own creations independently without listing fees or arbitrary restrictions. It is also intended to make life essentials available at lower prices, including free access where appropriate, based on income recognised by Butter Bank. The brief describes product verification before publication, seller-union membership, JaMail organisation accounts and pickup/delivery through UnterSous. These are distinct public services with distinct stated roles; this document does not claim that their technical integrations are already operational.

The stated relationship is that HeLLP owns Sweet Shop: Survival Service, and Sweet Shop: Digital Pantry is under Sweet Shop: Survival Service. These names and relationships are stated separately. The grant applicant must be identified from the relevant legal and organisational records; the project/service names alone do not establish the applicant's registered legal identity or tax classification.

## Most relevant grant model: Healthy Food Financing Initiative (HFFI) FARE Fund

Programme: https://www.investinginfood.com/fare-fund-2026/

The 2026 grant and technical-assistance application period is closed. Funding inquiries closed July 31, 2026, and invited full applications were due October 30, 2026. The programme offered grants of $20,000–$250,000 for planning and implementation and technical assistance up to $75,000.

This is the closest identified programme to Digital Pantry's affordable-essentials and food-access purpose. It supports eligible food retailers and post-harvest food supply-chain enterprises serving underserved areas. However, it is not a general marketplace grant: projects must demonstrate availability of an assortment of staple and perishable foods for retail sale at SNAP-authorised retailers in eligible underserved areas. The programme's published eligibility and project requirements must be checked against an online-only, multi-seller marketplace before treating it as eligible. The 2026 cycle also required applicants to complete a funding inquiry before submitting a full application.

Action: monitor the official FARE Fund page for a future grant/technical-assistance cycle and ask the programme team whether an online multi-seller retailer model can qualify, what site-control evidence is required, and how the marketplace's SNAP retailer status must be structured. Do not apply to the loan programme: Digital Pantry's funding request excludes debt.

## Next-cycle possibility: USDA Local Food Promotion Program

Official programme: https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/grants/lfpp

The FY2026 application period is closed; applications were due June 5, 2026. The programme offers planning, implementation, marketing/promotion and recruitment/training project types. The FY2026 ranges included $25,000–$100,000 for planning and $100,000–$500,000 for implementation. Eligible applicants include nonprofit and public-benefit corporations, cooperatives, producer networks, food councils and other listed entities. The programme supports local and regional food business enterprises that process, distribute, aggregate or store locally or regionally produced food. A cost share equal to 25% of the federal grant amount is required.

Potential fit: a defined local/regional food distribution and market-access component connecting qualifying local food producers with buyers. It is not funding for the entire general-purpose marketplace or unrelated products. Monitor for the next notice and confirm that Digital Pantry's actual activity and applicant type qualify.

## Next-cycle possibility: USDA Rural Business Development Grants (New York)

Official New York programme page: https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/business-programs/rural-business-development-grants/ny

The FY2026 application period closed June 30, 2026. Eligible applicants include public bodies, federally recognised Tribes and nonprofit entities primarily serving rural areas. There is no programme-wide matching requirement. Eligible activities can include technology-based economic development, feasibility studies, entrepreneur training, rural business incubators and strategic planning.

Potential fit: a rural seller-access, small-business development or seller-training component that benefits eligible rural areas. This is a future-cycle lead, not currently available funding. Contact New York USDA Rural Development for the next application schedule and confirm service-area eligibility.

## Conditional technology-development programme: EDA FY2027 Build to Scale

Official programme: https://www.eda.gov/funding/funding-opportunities/fiscal-year-2027-build-scale
Official FAQ: https://www.eda.gov/funding/programs/build-to-scale/2027/frequently-asked-questions

Open; deadline February 26, 2027 at 4:59 p.m. Eastern. Emerging Path awards can provide up to $1 million over 36 months; Growth Path awards up to $7 million over 48 months. The programme is designed to strengthen regional technology-commercialisation ecosystems and requires alignment with specified critical technology areas, such as machine learning, robotics, biotechnology, cybersecurity, advanced energy or advanced materials.

A nonprofit applicant must have support from a state or political subdivision of a state, documented by a qualifying resolution or letter. A 501(c)(3) designation is not mandatory, but the applicant must document nonprofit status, including articles of incorporation, a certificate of good standing and bylaws. The required match is 1:1: at least one dollar of committed, available and unrestricted cash or eligible in-kind match for each federal dollar. The FAQ says match cannot be waived. SAM.gov registration must be active before submission.

Fit: weak for a standalone online marketplace. It could only be considered if the proposal genuinely creates a regional critical-technology commercialisation programme with measurable ecosystem outcomes, eligible public support and the full match. It is not general marketplace development funding. EDA's FAQ also excludes several categories of expenditure, including advertising, cloud service credits, construction, equity investments, loans and participant-firm subsidies.

## HFFI Local and Regional Partnerships Program: future-cycle possibility

Official programme: https://www.investinginfood.com/partnerships-program/

The 2026 application period closed September 18, 2026. That cycle offered $20 million in grants to eligible regional, state or local public-private partnerships implementing food-financing programmes in underserved communities. A partnership must contain at least two entities, including at least one public entity. Capacity-building grants ranged from $200,000 to $1 million; credit-enhancement grants ranged from $500,000 to $3 million.

Potential fit: a future capacity-building application only if an eligible public-private partnership is formed and the work meets the programme's food-access requirements. This is not a direct individual-project grant to Digital Pantry, and credit-enhancement funding is not a substitute for unrestricted cash. Monitor for another grant cycle; do not pursue debt.

It is for eligible food banks, emergency food programmes and municipalities providing food relief, with capital uses such as facilities, cold storage, freezers and food-transport vehicles. It is not a software or general marketplace grant. Official information: https://www.dasny.org/about/what-we-do/grants-administration
- NY BRICKS Round 2: open through December 7, 2026, but it funds construction, renovation and modernisation of community centres. It does not fund an online marketplace or its software. Official information: https://www.dasny.org/about/what-we-do/grants-administration
- NYSERDA Commercialization Cohort: the current solicitation funds administrators of climate-technology accelerators, not a general retail marketplace. It is not a fit absent a qualifying climate-tech accelerator proposal. Official information: https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/funding-opportunities/current-funding-opportunities

## SNAP retailer authorisation: a separate operating requirement

Official online retailer requirements: https://fns-prod.azureedge.us/snap/retailer/online/requirements

SNAP retailer authorisation is free, but it is not development funding and does not globally register an online retailer. A retailer must first be eligible and authorised to accept SNAP, then complete the separate online-retailer authorisation process. The online storefront must support secure EBT PIN entry through an approved processor, eligible-food restrictions, refunds, split tender and required testing. SNAP applies to eligible food purchases, not all life essentials or all marketplace categories.

## Seller-union and nonprofit classification

IRS guidance on 501(c)(6): https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/business-leagues

A 501(c)(6) business league generally promotes the common business interests of a line of business. Its activities must improve business conditions rather than primarily provide particular services to individual persons. The IRS states that an organisation primarily engaging in a regular business ordinarily carried on for profit does not qualify, even if the activity is cooperative or self-sustaining.

The seller-union concept and operation of the marketplace raise distinct classification questions. Do not assume that 501(c)(6) status automatically covers the marketplace's retail or marketplace activity. The actual applicant's purposes, governance, member benefits, revenue model and activities need to be assessed before relying on a tax classification.

## Technology credits are not cash grants

- AWS Nonprofit Credit Program: https://aws.amazon.com/government-education/nonprofits/nonprofit-credit-program/ . AWS lists specified tax-exempt categories; 501(c)(6) is not among the listed US categories. For US organisations, AWS directs applicants through TechSoup and partner NGOs. Credits reduce infrastructure costs but are not cash.
- GitHub for Nonprofits: https://github.com/solutions/industry/nonprofits . The programme is limited to verified 501(c)(3) or equivalent organisations meeting GitHub's stated conditions. It provides discounted or free GitHub services, not cash. Eligibility must be checked against the actual applicant's status.

## How to pursue funding

1. Identify the registered legal applicant that will apply and administer any award. Use its actual registration name and status; do not infer them from the stated HeLLP–Survival Service–Digital Pantry relationship.
2. Prepare a fundable project scope around the actual Digital Pantry functions: seller onboarding and dashboards, product testing and verification, catalogue and inventory, buyer checkout, income-adjusted essential-goods access, secure payment handling, multi-seller order handling, delivery-cost calculation at checkout, and driver pickup and delivery through UnterSous; seller/worker compensation controls.
3. Separate the food-access component from the wider marketplace. This allows a food-specific grant application to describe qualifying staple and perishable foods without pretending that every product category qualifies.
4. Build an evidence package: current store links, examples of products, seller interest, buyer demand, intended service area, price comparisons, delivery model, product-verification process, income-recognition design, and measurable outcomes such as participating sellers, essential-goods orders, delivery costs and price reductions.
5. Prepare a project budget with itemised software development, security, privacy, accessibility, payment-processing, product verification, delivery coordination, hosting, testing and maintenance costs. Match each budget item to the proposed funder's allowable-cost rules.
6. For federal grants, confirm the applicant's legal eligibility and complete SAM.gov registration and any required federal application registrations early. For New York State grants, check the Statewide Financial System and any programme-specific prequalification requirements.
7. Prioritise future HFFI FARE Fund grants/technical assistance and USDA rural-development or local-food programmes where the actual project scope meets their rules. Pursue EDA Build to Scale only if its critical-technology, regional-ecosystem, public-support and 1:1 match requirements can genuinely be met.
8. Do not count loans, equity, donations, advertising, subscriptions or unconfirmed awards as secured funding. No currently open opportunity identified in this review directly pays for the entire general-purpose Digital Pantry marketplace without material restrictions.

There is no single global nonprofit or online-retailer registration. Recognition, incorporation, tax treatment, retailer authorisation, consumer protection, product safety, privacy, payments, imports and employment requirements are jurisdiction-specific.
