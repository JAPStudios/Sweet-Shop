# International registration and U.S. SNAP online-retailer pathway

Research date: 2026-11-26

## Stated project relationship

The supplied project relationship is: Sweet Shop: Digital Pantry is under Sweet Shop: Survival Service, which is owned by major not-for-profit organisation HeLLP. This document preserves those names and that stated relationship. It does not assume that a particular legal entity, tax classification or jurisdiction has already been verified.

## There is no single global registration

There is no universal filing that registers one online retailer or nonprofit organisation in every country. Legal formation and authority to operate are determined by each jurisdiction. Depending on where the organisation is established and where it carries on activities, separate requirements may apply to:

- legal formation and nonprofit recognition;
- registration of an overseas entity or branch;
- tax registration and reporting;
- online retail, consumer protection and product-safety rules;
- food handling, labelling and traceability;
- payment services and financial regulation;
- privacy and personal-data processing;
- employment, worker compensation and incarcerated-worker programmes;
- customs, imports and sales taxes.

The first required fact for a jurisdiction-specific filing plan is the country and subnational jurisdiction in which HeLLP is legally registered. The governing documents must then be checked to determine the legal mechanism by which HeLLP controls or owns Sweet Shop: Survival Service and how Digital Pantry is constituted under it. Nonprofit entities do not necessarily have share capital or conventional corporate ownership; the legal mechanism may instead involve membership, appointment rights, governing-body control or another arrangement permitted by the relevant law. The stated relationship should not be silently rewritten, but its legal implementation must match the governing jurisdiction.

For each additional country, determine whether Digital Pantry can operate through the existing legal applicant, must register as a foreign entity, or needs a locally constituted affiliate or other authorised operator. This decision depends on the local law and the actual operational footprint; a website being accessible in a country does not, by itself, establish every registration obligation.

## United States: formation and federal tax status are separate

If the intended legal applicant is formed in New York, domestic not-for-profit incorporation is done by filing a Certificate of Incorporation with the New York Department of State. The Department's current instructions list a $75 filing fee. This is a New York formation step, not a worldwide registration and not an IRS tax-exemption determination.

Official New York instructions:
https://dos.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/04/1511-inst.pdf

A federal exemption under Internal Revenue Code section 501(c)(6) is a tax classification, not a corporate formation jurisdiction. The IRS describes a business league as an association of persons with a common business interest whose purpose is to promote that interest rather than operate a regular business ordinarily carried on for profit. Activities must improve business conditions for a line of business, rather than primarily provide particular services to individual persons. The IRS states that an organisation primarily operating a regular business ordinarily carried on for profit does not qualify, even if the business is conducted cooperatively or only earns enough to be self-sustaining.

Digital Pantry's seller-union concept is relevant to evaluating a possible business-league purpose, but it does not establish eligibility. Its direct operation of a multi-seller marketplace and provision of services to buyers and sellers create material classification questions. The purposes, governance, membership, benefits, revenue model and actual activities of the legal applicant must be assessed before selecting a tax classification. Do not describe 501(c)(6) as a global nonprofit registration.

## United States: SNAP online retailer authorisation

SNAP authorisation is a United States retailer programme. It does not register Digital Pantry in other countries and it is not development funding.

The federal Food and Nutrition Administration (FNA) states that a retailer must first be eligible and authorised to accept SNAP. A retailer that wants to accept SNAP online must then obtain separate internet-retailer authorisation and must already have an online ecommerce presence.

For an online-only applicant, the published onboarding route is:

1. Email SNAPOnline@usda.gov to express interest and state that the application is for an online-only retailer. The online-only route requires a letter of intent.
2. Receive and complete the FNA internet-retailer application package.
3. Work with FNA on the business-requirements documentation and demonstration if requested.
4. Update the website/mobile storefront to meet the technical and programme requirements.
5. Complete end-to-end testing with FNA before launch.

The online platform must support secure EBT PIN entry through a third-party processor API, appropriate error handling, SNAP refunds, variable or estimated item pricing where applicable, one EBT card per customer account, eligible-food restrictions and split-tender payments. The current FNA page names Fiserv, Worldpay and Forage as approved online processors, while also explaining that other processors may be considered if they meet the technical requirements.

SNAP benefits apply only to eligible food purchases. They do not pay for all marketplace goods, general household essentials, shipping or every service charge.

A key unresolved point for Digital Pantry is the retailer-of-record model. The published requirements do not establish that a multi-seller marketplace automatically qualifies as the SNAP retailer merely because it hosts third-party sellers. Before designing the EBT checkout, obtain a written determination from FNA on whether the proposed marketplace can be authorised as the retailer of record, what inventory/control requirements apply to an online-only marketplace, and whether individual sellers would need separate authorisations. Do not represent SNAP acceptance as approved until FNA authorises the actual retailer and the platform passes testing.

## Application and compliance sequence

1. Confirm HeLLP's exact legal registration jurisdiction and obtain its current governing documents and registration evidence.
2. Identify the legal applicant that will operate or control Sweet Shop: Survival Service and Digital Pantry, and verify the mechanism that implements the stated relationship.
3. Obtain jurisdiction-specific advice on nonprofit formation, control, tax treatment and foreign-entity registration before filing.
4. Map each intended operating country and identify local retail, consumer, product, food, privacy, payment, employment and tax requirements.
5. Treat U.S. SNAP as a separate U.S. programme and resolve the marketplace retailer-of-record question with FNA before implementing EBT.
6. Maintain separate evidence for legal formation, nonprofit/tax status, retailer permits and programme-specific approvals. One does not substitute for another.
